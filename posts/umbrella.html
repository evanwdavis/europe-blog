Further Adventures of One Worthless Umbrella

by Hannah

Remember that magical umbrella that the nice man in the street promised would solve global warming and bring clean drinking water to Africa?

Besides the fact that it never did turn into a helicopter, it had several other major flaws.

The first I noticed as I walked through Florence on one slightly stormy day…

Two drops of rain fall…  So far so good…

Five drops of rain fall…  I think I notice something…

Nine drops of water… no, it can’t be…

Yes ladies and gentlemen.  By the time any real rain has fallen, the umbrella it has sagged under the enormous weight of 14 drops of water.

With my 3 Euro umbrella now around my ears a few inches from the base, Evan proceeds to die of laughter.  Too stubborn to mention that my umbrella can’t even stand up to its most basic function: stay up while rain hits it, I decide to pretend that short based umbrellas are totally the rage.

But Vienna is far worse.  It is in windy Vienna that I realize that this umbrella could not withstand a baby’s breath without flipping out.  This leads to all manner of attempted solutions, all of which result in me being wet and/or hitting things.  After numerous minutes of franticly trying various methods of making this stupid piece of cloth and metal actually work, I came up with three superb solutions…

The Battering Ram:

Despite the fact that it offers no protection from the rain, my umbrella stays right side up, and I get to play the fun game “guess which direction the wind will come from next!”  Hours of entertainment!

But realizing that I’m now soaked, I decide to opt for the “parachuter” look by firmly grasping each side of the umbrella.

But that just looked stupid…

Finally I give up and resign myself to having an inside out umbrella.  A logical solution seems to be to use this worthless umbrella as a bat to hit away every droplet.

(Disclaimer: After experimentation, I have discovered that randomly swatting at rain droplets in crowded streets is not the best way to make friends…)

And then, under all of the stress and apprehension of the umbrella actually working for one second, it decides that all of its metal spines should start breaking…

Maybe I should get a new umbrella?

Naaaaaah
