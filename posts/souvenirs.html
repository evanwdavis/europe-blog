A Lesson From a Dumb Traveler

by Hannah

“Pack Light!”

These are usually the words that start every chapter of every travel book (and are then repeated numerous times throughout the following chapters).  I think it’s safe to assume that Rick Steves has it tattooed on his arm.

Being savvy guidebook consumers ourselves, we were well aware of the pack light mantra and were determined to do laundry in the sink with pride in order to keep our bags small.

After a few days of cutting out an electronic here and an extra shirt there, we were pretty proud of our one small backpack and carry-on duffel a piece.  Walking up to the airport terminal, we joined the other “travel fashionistas,” aka everyone wearing as many items on the flight over as they can so they don’t have to check luggage.

(As an aside, I think the increase in the cost of checking luggage has certainly had an effect on the way people travel.  Standing in the security checkpoint line, I began to be aware of how many more people were attempting to cram just as much stuff as they normally would check into their carry on luggage.  Bags bursting at the seams were joined with the most ridiculous array of “personal items.” I saw several people, usually women, arguing with the TSA officials that they always wore a cat carrier on their head and it just simply should not be considered an additional item.  And no, they would not be checking their massage table and matching lamp set because they had doctor’s note.  My favorite was a younger man who had roped together several bags and was claiming that it only counted as one.)

Yet law-abiding as always… usually… Evan… we stuck to the rules and happily carried our half empty backpacks and light duffels to the gate.  We were ready to take Europe by storm, navigate cobblestone streets without wheels, and hike to our 4th floor elevator-less hotel room without worry.

And then we made a mistake.  A fatal mistake.  This was to packing light what the Battle of Stalingrad was to Hitler (sorry, we’ve been to a lot of museums on this trip).

We bought souvenirs.

Heavy souvenirs.

In our first week.

Now it is fun to buy souvenirs.  A t-shirt here, a bracelet there.  A Vietnamese painting that you ship home using the entirety of your 21 year old savings account.  Little things.

For example, my family has a fine magnet collection. One that I am convinced will one day be museum worthy- especially the Eiffel tower man carrying a baguette: priceless.

But the great thing about magnets, and bracelets, and t-shirts is that they are relatively small and light.  And usually you only have to cart them around for a week before dispersing them among your friends and family.  Unfortunately, being in Italy at the very beginning of the trip, and finding it the most wonderful place to find amazing wines, olive oils, and wooden products, I decide to buy them all as gifts (and a few for myself) not realizing that I am now stuck with my purchases for another 3 1/2 weeks.

Suddenly my delightfully light backpack was weighed down with all of my fun finds.  Finds made of heavy glass and wood.  For 3 1/2 weeks.  On my back.  I don’t think I could have found more dense, unsuitable-for-travel items if I had tried.  I might as well have packed an oak table for the heck of it.  Although I was considering that life size marble replica of Michelangelo’s David.

Bonding at the bus stop.

Sharing all of those wonderful moments of travel.

On second thought, adding the statue to my load does not sound appealing…


Maybe Evan would have carried it.  Can we go back?
