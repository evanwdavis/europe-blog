Hannah · Barcelona

Food, Glorious Food (Part I)

by Hannah

I'm sure almost any one of you could have guessed that at least one food post was coming. After all, most people I know have been subjected, at one time or another, to my experimentations with new recipes and foods (not always successful — note to readers: you can't "wing" vegan crab cakes... you have to follow the instructions very carefully). My sorority house received the brunt of it, as cooking was my very own form of procrastination. Some people go for runs, watch tv, or take a nap when they don't want to write that paper... I plan out a 4 course meal for whoever would happen to be downstairs at the moment (and hungry). In fact, my newest kindred spirit and I bonded over a love of all things delicious, healthy, and fresh (and the fact that Narissa and I are pretty much the same person. It's creepy).

So the opportunity to visit all of these new places and explore their markets and restaurants set my little foodie heart aflutter. I am proud to report back that so far, Europe has not disappointed.

The Boqueria Market, Barcelona

Such a beautiful place. Located in el Raval neighborhood near Las Ramblas, this market first opened in 1840. According to the sign at the entrance, it is one of Barcelona's oldest and most traditional markets (and where many of Barcelona's greatest chefs shop for their ingredients). For me, it was a religious experience.

Walking into the market I was first overwhelmed with the colors. Mind you there's nothing plastic, no packaging, brands, stacks of "4 for $5" boxes of zebra cakes, or flashy cereal boxes. But there are amazing, vibrant colors everywhere. The dark purple of small round eggplants tucked in wooden baskets, the bright ruby red of a thousand juicy cherries that cascade across the counter, the entire spectrum of green spread among one small stand packed with 40 types of leafy vegetables, oranges, blues, pinks, yellows, whites, browns and colors I had never seen in produce before. It was a sensory overload, and it took me a few minutes just to calm down and keep my eyes from jumping uncontrollably from one small stand to the other, distracted by employees wheeling big crates teeming with citrus between the rows.

Taking a few steps in you start to distinguish the complex swirl of aromas. Skilled stall owners chop through fresh juicy fruits in seconds sending droplets of juice (and the accompanying sweet odor) into the air. There it meets the subtle earth scents of radishes and carrots just removed from the ground before mixing with the salty smells of cured meats and Barcelona's famous Iberian Ham (much like traditional Virginian ham). What surprised me the most, though, was the absence of an odor from the seafood. The place was teeming with huge fish, bunches of crabs, lobsters, shrimp, and salmon being filleted into steaks right in front of your eyes, yet my nose couldn't tell that it was 1 foot away from one hundred pounds of seafood. It was that fresh. What the Vietnamese specialty rotten fish paste was to one extreme of the odor spectrum, this seafood was at the other.

Evan and I both wished we had a kitchen so that we could really take advantage of the market, but realizing we had turned a 30 minute pack-a-lunch stop into two hours of gaping with our mouths open (with a fair amount of drooling), we figured we should grab a few things and continue with our very busy day. I wandered from stall to stall checking prices and quality (which Evan compared to trying to choose between a Lamborghini or a Ferrari — you can't really pick the wrong one). We ended up with some fresh baguettes we saw the woman pull out of the oven, a heap of delicious red cherries, a bag of prewashed herb and lettuce mix (before we heard about the E. Coli problem — check back in 12 days to see if we're still here), a big hunk of Manchego cheese, one package of Serrano ham, and two fresh squeezed fruit juices for breakfast. We made a picnic outside of the Botanical Gardens on Montjuic hill and had quite the feast.
