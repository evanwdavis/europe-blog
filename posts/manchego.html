Hannah · Barcelona

The Manchego Cheese Index

by Hannah

So you've heard of the McDonald's Big Mac Index. The idea that you can estimate the value of a currency by comparing the price of McDonald's hamburgers around the world and therefore determine whether the currency is over- or undervalued.

Well watch out world. The latest craze to push burgernomics into the past is the Manchego Cheese Index. (I am waiting for the Economist to call for rights to this amazing bit of data. Any day now...)

What, might you ask, is Manchego Cheese? Ah, it is semi-cured, ivory colored, sheep's milk deliciousness from La Mancha. [Insert unoriginal Don Quixote joke below]

But it has a far cooler purpose than just giving rise to drawing cartoon sheep in funny poses (though that alone is quite grand). Through my endless quest for Manchego cheese at every meal and in every grocery store, I soon became quite familiar with the prices and relative markups. After very detailed data collection and with an estimated error range of 0-100%, I proudly present the Manchego Cheese Index, an invaluable tool for all tourists to determine their purchasing power at any given location in Barcelona.

(Disclosure: I did not properly account for any variables. Size of portion, age of cheese, quality of cheese, time of day, seasonality, restaurant overhead, accompanying garnish, whether the plate was round or square, and all other relevant independent and dependent factors were happily ignored.

In honor of all of my amazing professors in the business school and econ department at W&M, I wouldn't want anyone to perceive the worthlessness of this index as a failing of their excellent instruction. You can't blame them, I did just insert a sheep riding a horse two paragraphs above this.)

Now that my alma mater is quite safe from ridicule and I can (hopefully) still visit the finance department, I will continue.

Barrio Gotica — The Gothic Quarter

Strolling the lovely windy streets and hidden alley gardens of el barrio gotica, you stumble upon a wide assortment of tapas bars and patio eateries. Though close to Las Ramblas, this neighborhood seems somewhat secluded from the bustle of street vendors and cheap souvenir stands that have overtaken other touristy places and is a nice break from frantically clutching your purse.

In a lovely plaza seated at a shady table for two, you can expect to pay 3.50 Euros for one tapas plate of Manchego cheese.

Barceloneta Beach

After a wonderful day hiking the area surrounding Montserrat in the mountains, we headed back to the city for some R&R (aka another 2 hour walk through the beach area).

Barceloneta may not be the prettiest beach in Barcelona, but it is certainly the place to strut your stuff amid beach stands like "Vegas" and hot clubs like IceBarcelona (ok, maybe hot was the wrong word), where really cool people can get even cooler by paying 20 Euros to stand in subzero temperatures in parkas.

In this wondrous land of nightclubs, rollerblades, and yucky public bathrooms, expect to pay heftily for the privilege to sit in the sand, back to the water, listening to katy perry remixes.

One plate of Manchego cheese is 8.90 Euros. The experience of flying all the way to Spain to be at AnyBeachStand, USA: priceless.

The Picasso Museum (Most Southern Point of Barrio Gotica)

Funny thing about finding museums in Barcelona is that they are heavily advertised when you are a 20 minute walk away, but get anywhere near them and you might as well be looking for signs to the nearest time machine. You're not going to find anything. So in order to rate the Manchego Cheese Index for famous attractions, we must find them first. Even something as heavily visited as the Picasso Museum is relatively unmarked, so the brave tourist must look for other signs as to its location. Luckily for us, the well trained souvenir eye can spot subtle differences in product ratios to pinpoint tourist attraction locations.

This starts with understanding the four most prevalent types of souvenirs.

Once the basic product types are identified, the tourist must then learn the metamorphosis process by which any cheap souvenir can become an attraction specific souvenir.

Furthermore, the experienced visitor will see how even the more interactive tourist experiences adapt to their surroundings.

Armed with this knowledge, a tourist taps into their ancestor's tracking skills.

Using our ancient hunter ways, we finally stumbled upon the Picasso Museum (correction, we finally stumbled upon the line, which inevitably led us to the ticket counter) and a quaint little plaza with stores called "Picasso Cafe," "Picasso Bar," and my personal favorite "Picasso's Hotel and Laundromat." Sure was a busy guy.

Right around the corner we found a restaurant boasting Manchego cheese for 3.50 Euros. Surprised? I was too. And then I realized that there would be no way of finding my way back to the Museum, so it didn't really count as a "tourist location."

Ciutdad Park

The main city park features a pretty waterfall, the Spanish Arc of Triumph, several dilapidated old greenhouses, and the entrance to the zoo and is about as un-touristy as it gets. There were no fake bags for sale, no pastel colored Ray Bans, and (worst of all) not a single tour guide waving a giant wad of purple material on a stick. Swallowing my fierce disappointment (what was I supposed to do without Barcelona bathrobes to look at?) I resigned myself to strolling the park, and like, looking around. I was almost hopeful after seeing a group of young men and women doing tricks on a tightrope they had set up, but, after realizing that they weren't looking for money, I returned grumpily to just enjoying the beautiful day.

Right outside the park we found a small cafe: 1 Euro for a half portion of Manchego Cheese. I was afraid if I ate there I would be kicked out of the country for not being touristy enough, so we ducked our heads and hurried by, promising that we would be sure to buy the next Dali melted clock poster we found.

Las Ramblas del Mar

Ah finally. A place that knows what I want. That I will happily pay 15x the amount I should if a restaurant puts a pretty fake bush in front of my table so it looks "exclusive." A place that welcomes the exhausted tourist who cannot handle the hardship of any more authentic experiences and would have preferred that the entire trip be from within a giant bubble, or even better a simulation they could ride at home. That travel is not about pushing my personal boundaries and accepting a wider view of the world, but more about checking off my Around the World bingo card. That all I really want in life is a properly translated English menu and a beer I recognize.

Welcome to las Ramblas del Mar.

Now I am the first to admit that there are times that I just want to be home. Sometimes I am tired of losing foreign language roulette and having three meals in a row of rabbit fat stew (I swear the words looked different). I am tired of my phone not working and the fact that I've worn the same outfit 7 days in a row for the benefit of saving 4 pounds of luggage. But for all of those times I wish I was home, the answer for me has never been to let someone else take advantage of my vacation time and resources. And that is what Las Ramblas del Mar seems to excel at.

Formally a small port and the beginning of the stretch of cruise docking points, Las Ramblas del Mar is now a tourist haven (with a few locals taking advantage of the children's park) with an aquarium, indoor shopping mall, an IMAX theatre, and all of the massive types of chain restaurants you know and love (with a token Spanish flag in the background so you remember you're in Spain). With views of the water (if you can see past your exclusive shrubbery), the lucky cruise ship passenger or burger eating family of four can try some authentic Spanish Manchego cheese for 10.50 Euros. Ouch.
